# Milestone 5 complete storage reconciliation matrix

This audit covers every accepted `hr1:` key family in `docs/design/storage.md` and every currently registered Lua transition in `internal/valkey/scripts.go`. It classifies authority, records lifecycle ownership, describes startup/recovery behavior, and identifies the additional contracts selected for Milestone 5 tickets 03–04. There are 26 accepted key families and 31 registered transitions.

## Classification vocabulary

- **Authoritative:** state cannot be reconstructed safely from another persisted record. Invalid or missing data is held or isolated; it is never guessed.
- **Derived:** rebuildable from authoritative state. Startup/recovery may restore, rescore, or remove it.
- **Disposable:** invalid state can be deleted safely because deletion only forces re-authentication or natural expiry.
- **Idempotency-only:** retained only to replay an operation result or fence a repeated request. It is not used to reconstruct business state.
- **Audit-only:** append-only accountability data. Missing historical events cannot be reconstructed by reconciliation.

## Selected Milestone 5 additions

The audit found two bounded hardening groups. They are specified in `.scratch/milestone-5/spec.md` before implementation.

1. **Ticket 03 — active Delivery Attempt cross-link validation.** Validate current `leased` heads against their `delivery_token_digest`, `hr1:t:<digest>`, and claim `hr1:op:<operation_id>` record. Never hash, recover, or replace the plaintext token. Derived deadline score remains repairable; mismatched authoritative active-attempt records create a Recipient block. The idempotent claim response retains its original deadline after extension, bounded by claimed time and the current head/token deadline. Terminal token records and completed extend operation records remain TTL-bound and are not globally scanned.
2. **Ticket 04 — message lifecycle validation.** Semantically validate queued/dead-letter Canonical Message blobs, metadata, attempt history, block markers, and compact success records; detect mutually exclusive live states. Queue-local ambiguity creates a Recipient block; dead-letter/global ambiguity holds readiness. Only provably orphaned metadata/history is removed, and no dedup record, payload, attempt, replay pair, or success record is reconstructed.

## Rejected automatic repairs

- **Recreate a missing dedup record from `hr1:mi`: rejected.** Absence can be intentional TTL expiry or early eviction; restoring it would extend duplicate suppression beyond policy.
- **Recreate a missing Canonical Message from queue, history, metadata, or audit: rejected.** None contains the payload.
- **Reset a queued replay to cycle 1/attempt 1: rejected.** It would duplicate or reorder Delivery Attempts.
- **Recreate attempt history from head state or token records: rejected.** Completed outcome, reason, consumer metadata, and timing are not recoverable.
- **Recreate a Delivery Token or claim operation from a lease: rejected.** The token is secret and non-derivable; inventing one breaks fencing and claim replay.
- **Recreate compact success metadata: rejected.** The payload and full history have already been deleted; acknowledgement evidence is incomplete.
- **Create a Recipient block for missing dead-letter payload/history: rejected.** Clearing the block only verifies active queue state and cannot certify DLQ integrity.
- **Fabricate mandatory audit entries from observed desired state: rejected.** Observed state cannot prove which request performed the mutation or whether the original audit append occurred.
- **Delete malformed authoritative endpoint, queue, DLQ, or audit state automatically: rejected.** Destructive repair would erase incident evidence and may break ordering/accountability.
